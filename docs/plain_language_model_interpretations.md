# Plain-Language Interpretation of the Refreshed Models

These interpretations are based on the current coefficient tables in `outputs/models/*_coefficients.csv` after switching the active negotiator-status baseline to the control-labeled condition and rerunning H1, H2, and H3.

Higher judgment scores mean less severe moral condemnation. Lower judgment scores mean harsher moral condemnation.

Terms are interpreted only when `p < .10`. Otherwise, the text states that there is no clear evidence.

## H1: Victim Subset

**Model translation**

This model examines how empathy, N1 status, N2 status, and harmful-deal acceptance are associated with moral judgment severity in the victim subset, while holding constant participant characteristics and repeated judgments from the same participant. For negotiator-status terms, the control-labeled condition is the baseline.

**Key significant effects**

- Individuals with higher perspective taking tend to judge the negotiator as less morally wrong. On average, a one-unit increase in perspective taking is associated with a substantial increase in judgment scores.
- When the judged negotiator accepts the harmful deal, judgments become much more severe. This is a substantial decrease in judgment scores.

**Non-significant findings**

- There is no clear evidence that fantasy, empathic concern, personal distress, N1 ingroup, N1 outgroup, N2 ingroup, N2 outgroup, participant faculty, sex, age, economic status, or negotiator slot is associated with moral judgment in this model.

**Interaction effects**

- This model does not include interaction terms.

**Random effects interpretation**

- No random-effects terms were estimated. Repeated judgments were handled with participant-clustered standard errors, so there is no separate participant- or scenario-level variance component to interpret.

## H3: Victim Subset

**Model translation**

This model examines whether the link between empathy and moral judgment changes across N1 status in the victim subset, while holding constant harmful-deal acceptance, decision-by-status terms, N2 status, and participant characteristics. The control-labeled N1 condition is the baseline for the judged-status contrasts.

**Key significant effects**

- When the judged negotiator accepts the harmful deal, judgments become much more severe. This is a substantial decrease in judgment scores.

**Non-significant findings**

- There is no clear evidence that empathy has a standalone main effect in this model once the interaction terms are included.
- There is no clear evidence that N1 ingroup, N1 outgroup, the decision-by-status terms, N2 status, participant faculty, sex, age, economic status, or negotiator slot is associated with moral judgment in this model.

**Interaction effects**

- The effect of perspective taking depends on the judged negotiator's status.
- Specifically, the positive association between perspective taking and judgment becomes stronger, and shifts toward less severe condemnation, when N1 is ingroup rather than in the control baseline.
- The positive association between perspective taking and judgment also becomes stronger, and shifts toward less severe condemnation, when N1 is outgroup rather than in the control baseline.
- There is no clear evidence for the remaining empathy-by-status interactions.

**Random effects interpretation**

- No random-effects terms were estimated. Repeated judgments were handled with participant-clustered standard errors, so there is no separate participant- or scenario-level variance component to interpret.
